Required thematic courses in philosophy of language, epistemology and metaphysics, with possible electives in philosophy of mind and philosophy of science.

Work at the research frontier

Focused courses on contemporary debates, such as epistemic injustice, modality, evaluative language, mental properties and metaphysical explanation.

Develop and defend an original argument

Write a focused, problem-driven thesis with supervision, draft seminars, oral defence and formal opposition on a fellow student's work.

Frequently asked questions

What prospective students need to know.

When will the programme begin?

The full-scale programme is planned for 2027. Exact application dates, entry requirements and the first intake date will be published once formally confirmed.

Is the programme entirely online?

Yes. The planned format is fully online, with no required campus attendance. Most teaching is asynchronous, supplemented by optional live sessions and selected collaborative activities.

Can I study part-time?

Yes. The programme is planned in both full-time and part-time formats. The full-time route is designed for completion in two years, while the part-time route extends over four years.

What areas of philosophy does the programme cover?

The core focus is analytic theoretical philosophy, especially metaphysics, epistemology and philosophy of language. Elective space may also include philosophy of mind, philosophy of science and related subjects.

How will I be assessed?

Assessment will combine substantial written work, oral presentations or examinations, ongoing tasks and feedback on drafts. The thesis includes supervision, seminars, defence and opposition.

Who is the programme for?

It is intended for students with a strong previous background in philosophy who want advanced, research-connected study and may be considering doctoral work or analytically demanding careers.
